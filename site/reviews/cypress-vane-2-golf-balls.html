Cypress Vane 2 Golf Balls Review: Is It Worth It for Junior Golfers?

A $24 low-compression ball that spins on pitch shots and feels soft on the putter. Eight rounds and a lot of short game practice later, here's where it wins and where it doesn't.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated June 2026 ·6 min read

Quick Take

If you're a junior who loses a couple of balls a round and still wants something that checks on a pitch shot, this is the ball to buy by the dozen. It won't spin like a $50 tour ball on full wedges, and it feels rocky when it's cold — but at $24 it's the best trade-off I've found.

What It Is

The Vane 2 is Cypress's low-compression three-piece ball, aimed at players who don't swing it 110 mph. It sits in the middle of their line: softer than a distance ball, cheaper than a urethane tour ball.

How I Tested It

Eight rounds in May and June, plus about six short game sessions where I hit the same shots with the Vane 2 and with the tour ball I'd been playing. I also played three rounds with a single ball on purpose to see how the cover held up, and two rounds in cold, wet weather to check the feel.

What I Liked

  • It's soft off the putter face — a muted click instead of a loud tap. On fast greens that made speed easier to judge, and my lag putting genuinely got tighter over those eight rounds.
  • It actually checks inside 40 yards. One hop and a stop on a decent strike. That's the part I didn't expect at this price.
  • The alignment line is thin and long. Sounds like nothing. I use it on every single putt and I don't have to draw my own.
  • The cover holds up. I played the same ball for three rounds and it still looked fine apart from a scuff from a rake.
  • It's cheap enough that I stopped playing scared. There's a par 3 over water at my home course where I used to switch to a beat-up ball. I don't anymore.

What I Didn't Like

  • Full wedges release more than a tour ball. From 100+ yards, plan on an extra eight or ten feet of rollout. If your game is built on flying wedges to a number, you'll notice.
  • Cold weather kills the feel. Under about 50°F it goes from soft to rocky, and I lost noticeable distance in the two cold rounds.
  • The yellow ones scuff visibly fast. Purely cosmetic, but they look beat up before they play beat up.

Who It's For

Good Fit For

  • Juniors with driver speed under about 95 mph
  • Anyone losing two or more balls a round
  • Tournament players who want the same ball in play all season without the tour-ball price
  • Players working on short game who want feedback from pitch shots

Not a Great Fit For

  • High-speed players who want maximum spin on full wedges
  • Anyone who plays a lot of cold-weather golf
  • Players already scoring well with a urethane tour ball — no reason to switch

Price & Value

$24 a dozen, and I've seen it at $20 in three-dozen packs. The two things you'd realistically compare it to: a urethane tour ball at $45–$50, and a hard two-piece distance ball at around $18.

The distance ball is cheaper and I hated putting with it. The tour ball spins more on full wedges and I lose $4 every time it finds a creek. For a junior who is still losing golf balls, the middle option is the right one, and this is a good version of the middle option.

Final Verdict

4/5 — Very good. A genuinely good ball at a price a junior can actually sustain. It gives up real spin on full wedges and it doesn't like the cold, which is why it's a 4 — but for most junior golfers, this is the ball I'd put in the bag and stop thinking about.
