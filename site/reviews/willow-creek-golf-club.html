Willow Creek Golf Club Review: A Junior Golfer's Take

The best conditioned course I've played and a genuinely hard test — which is great on tournament day and a bad deal for a casual junior round. Played twice in competition, once as a public guest.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated April 2026 ·6 min read

Disclosure: My two competitive rounds here were part of a junior tournament entry fee. The third round I paid the public rate myself.

Quick Take

On tournament day this place is fantastic — pure greens, a real test, and a practice area that gets you ready. As a course a junior chooses to go play on a Tuesday, it's expensive, slow, and too long from the tees you'd actually use. Know which visit you're making.

The Basics

  • Where: Maple Ridge, Oregon. Semi-private — public tee times weekday afternoons only.
  • Par: 72. Yardage: 6,780 back, 6,050 middle, 5,400 forward. No dedicated junior tees.
  • Green fees: $75 public weekday afternoon. Junior: $40, but only when playing with a member.
  • Walkability: walkable, but cart-path-only most of the year and some long green-to-tee walks.
  • Practice: grass range, chipping green, practice bunker — all excellent.

What Stood Out

  • Conditioning is the best I've played. The greens ran around 11 during our qualifier and were pure — no bumps, no unrepaired marks, edge to edge.
  • It's a real test. Three par 4s over 420 from the middle tees and thick summer rough. If you can shoot a good number here you can shoot it anywhere in the area.
  • The pre-round practice area is tournament grade. Grass range, a chipping green you can actually get short-sided on, and a practice bunker with the same sand as the course.
  • No. 16 is the hole everyone talks about after the round — a par 3 over water to a green with a false front. I've made 2 and I've made 6 on it.

Challenges

  • $75 public is out of reach for most juniors paying with their own money.
  • Cart-path-only plus long walks between green and tee makes it a five-hour walk even in a group that's moving.
  • From the middle tees it's too long for most juniors under 14, and the forward tees still leave awkward forced carries on 4 and 12.
  • The welcome depends on the day. During events the staff are great with junior fields. Showing up as a weekday public teenager, I got a fairly long look in the pro shop before anyone said hello.

Junior-Friendliness

Honestly, limited — and that's the main thing holding the rating down.

  • Junior rate: only with a member, so for most juniors it doesn't exist.
  • Forward tees: present at 5,400, but they're women's tees, not junior tees. There's a real gap between "can reach a green in regulation" and what's on offer here.
  • Pace: five hours is a lot to ask of a younger player.
  • During tournaments: excellent — clear signage, range access included, staff who know what junior events need.

Best For

Show up for

  • Tournament prep the week before an event on a similar course
  • Playing in an actual junior event here
  • Older juniors (15+) who can handle 6,050 yards

Play somewhere else if

  • You want a casual round — you'll spend $75 to feel bad about your score
  • You're under 14 or under about 200 yards off the tee
  • You only have a whole day if it's spent walking a cart-path-only course

Final Verdict

3/5 — Solid, with trade-offs. A terrific tournament venue and a poor casual choice for a junior — which is exactly what a 3 means on this site. If your event is here, come early, use the practice area, and enjoy the best greens you'll putt all year. If you're spending your own $75 on a Tuesday, go play Ridgeline three times instead.
