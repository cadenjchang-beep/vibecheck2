Ridgeline Municipal Golf Course Review: A Junior Golfer's Take

A $12 junior twilight rate, greens that roll truer than the price suggests, and a starter who knows the high school team by name. Played eight times this year, always walking.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated June 2026 ·6 min read

Quick Take

If you're a junior in this area learning to play real golf on a real course, this is where you go. Twelve dollars after 2pm, greens that roll true, and staff who are genuinely glad you're there. Weekend morning pace is brutal and the bunkers are a coin flip — plan around both.

The Basics

  • Where: Cedar Falls, Oregon — about 20 minutes from downtown, city-owned.
  • Par: 71. Yardage: 6,240 from the back, 5,480 from the middle, 4,610 from the forward tees.
  • Green fees: $34 weekday adult / $42 weekend. Junior: $18 anytime, $12 after 2pm.
  • Walkability: Walkable, but holes 5 through 8 climb. Carts are $16 and optional.
  • Practice: Grass range on weekends, mats on weekdays. Big putting green. No chipping green.

What Stood Out

  • The $12 twilight junior rate is the best deal I know of. That's a real round on a real course for less than a large bucket at most places.
  • The greens roll true. They're not fast — maybe a 9 — but they're consistent green to green, which is honestly better for learning speed than fast-and-bumpy.
  • No. 7 is the best hole around here. A 310-yard downhill par 4 where you can genuinely have a go at the green. Half my team has a story about it.
  • The starter knows the junior players. Show up alone and he'll pair you with other juniors instead of sticking you behind a foursome.
  • It drains. I played in March rain and it wasn't a swamp, which around here is worth a lot.

Challenges

  • Weekend morning pace is rough — five hours is normal. After 2pm it's closer to 3:45.
  • Holes 5–8 climb hard. Carrying a bag in August, that stretch is a grind and my scores show it.
  • The bunkers are inconsistent. Some have four inches of soft sand, some are packed dirt. It's a bad place to learn bunker play, and I don't practice it here.
  • Blind tee shot on 13 with no aiming marker. First-timers lose a ball right almost every time. Aim at the tall fir.

Junior-Friendliness

This is the part Ridgeline gets right, and it's why I keep coming back.

  • Junior rate: yes, and it's a real discount — roughly half price, not $5 off.
  • Forward tees: marked and mowed at 4,610, which is a fair test for a 12-year-old.
  • Playing alone: juniors 12 and up can book and play unaccompanied.
  • Team practice: they give the high school teams range access at a reduced rate in season.
  • Vibe: nobody has ever made me feel like I'm taking a tee time from a paying adult.

Best For

Show up for

  • A casual round after school
  • Your first time playing a full 18 on a real course
  • Walking practice and building endurance
  • Juniors on their own money

Play somewhere else if

  • You're prepping for a tournament — the greens are too slow and the bunkers too inconsistent
  • You only have a weekend morning free and you hate five-hour rounds
  • You want to practice short game before you play (there's no chipping green)

Final Verdict

4/5 — Very good. For a casual junior round, Ridgeline is the best value I've played, and the staff make it somewhere you actually want to go back to. The pace on weekend mornings and the state of the bunkers keep it from being a 5. Book after 2pm, walk it, and it's close to perfect for what it costs.
