Northbank Talon GX2 Junior Driver Review: Is It Worth It for Junior Golfers?

An adjustable-length junior driver that grows with you — tested over 14 rounds and three tournaments by a 15-year-old who grew an inch in the middle of testing.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated July 2026 ·7 min read

Quick Take

This is the best junior driver I've tested this year for anyone still growing into their swing speed — the adjustable length is the whole story. It's not cheap, and it won't out-drive a full adult setup, but for ages 12–16 still building speed, it's the smartest buy in this price range.

What It Is

The Talon GX2 is a 12.5° driver from Northbank, a small brand that only makes junior clubs. The thing that makes it different: the shaft length adjusts from 40" to 43" in half-inch steps with a wrench, so one driver covers three or four years of growing.

How I Tested It

14 rounds and about a dozen range sessions between March and June — three of those rounds were junior tournaments. Weather went from 45°F and raining to 85°F and dry, which around here is the whole year in three months.

I started at 41" and moved to 42" in May after I grew about an inch. I compared it against the fixed-length junior driver I'd been playing and, on a few range sessions, against a teammate's adult 10.5° with a stiff shaft.

What I Liked

  • The length adjustment is real, not a gimmick. Half-inch steps, one wrench, five minutes on the kitchen floor. I grew mid-season and didn't have to ask my parents for a new driver.
  • It sounds like a real driver. A low, solid thud instead of the tinny click most junior drivers make. That matters more than it should when your whole team is standing on the tee behind you.
  • Low-face misses still go. My bad one is thin, and thin strikes still got in the air and went about 85% of my normal number instead of scuttling 90 yards.
  • It stays swingable when I'm tired. Head and shaft are both light. On the 34th hole of a 36-hole day I could still swing it at full speed, which was not true of the adult driver I tried.
  • The grip is actually junior-sized — a smaller diameter grip, not an adult grip cut down. My hands don't slide around on it in the rain.

What I Didn't Like

  • It launches high, and wind eats it. On a windy tournament day I hit 3-wood off two tees because the driver was ballooning and coming down short.
  • At 43" the stock shaft feels whippy. If you're already swinging in the 90s, you'll outgrow the shaft before you outgrow the length, and then you're paying for a re-shaft on top of the club.
  • $219 is a lot for something you might play for two or three years.
  • The headcover is junk. The magnet stopped closing after about a month and it now falls off in the trunk.

Who It's For

Good Fit For

  • Ages 12–16, roughly 5'0" to 5'8", still growing
  • Driver swing speed around 65–85 mph
  • Juniors who play enough to justify $200+ on one club
  • Anyone who's been playing a hand-me-down adult driver that's too heavy

Not a Great Fit For

  • Under about 11 — it's still heavy at 40"
  • Swing speed 95+ (the stock shaft won't hold up)
  • Players who are basically done growing — buy an adult driver instead
  • Someone who plays five rounds a summer

Price & Value

$219 at full price. To compare it to what you'd realistically look at instead: a fixed-length junior driver like the Harbourline J3 is about $139, and an entry-level adult driver is $250–$300.

The math depends on one thing — how much growing is left. If you'd otherwise buy two fixed-length juniors over three years, the Talon is cheaper and you never play a driver that's the wrong length in between. If your growth spurt is basically done, skip this and put the money toward an adult driver you'll keep for five years.

Final Verdict

4/5 — Very good. For a junior in the middle of growing, this is the driver I'd point a teammate at. The high flight and the whippy feel at full length are real limits, and they're the reason it's a 4 and not a 5 — but the adjustable length solves a problem that costs families real money, and it solves it well.
