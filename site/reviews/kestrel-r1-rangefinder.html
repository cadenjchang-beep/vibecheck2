Kestrel R1 Laser Rangefinder Review: Is It Worth It for Junior Golfers?

Fast, small, and simple enough to use when your hands are shaking on the first tee of a tournament. Five rounds of testing, including two competitive ones — and yes, this one was sent to me free.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated May 2026 ·6 min read

Disclosure: Kestrel sent me the R1 for free to review. They didn't see this before it went up and they didn't ask for any changes. My rating scale doesn't move for free gear — see the disclosure policy.

Quick Take

The fastest and simplest rangefinder I've used, and it's light enough that I forget it's in my pocket. The slope switch is easy to knock, which matters because most tournaments require slope to be turned off. Worth it if you play tournaments or unfamiliar courses — skip it if you play the same muni every week.

What It Is

The R1 is Kestrel's entry laser rangefinder: 6x magnification, flag lock with vibration, and a slope mode you can switch on and off. It's noticeably smaller than most lasers I've held.

How I Tested It

Five rounds over about six weeks, two of them junior tournament rounds where slope had to be switched off. Mostly flat gray Pacific Northwest light, which is where my old rangefinder's display used to wash out, plus one bright afternoon and one round in light rain.

I compared every reading against the sprinkler heads and against a teammate's GPS watch on 30-odd holes.

What I Liked

  • It locks in under a second on a flag at 150 yards. No hunting, no re-shooting, which keeps me from holding up the group.
  • The vibration when it locks means I trust the number. With my old one I'd shoot twice to be sure. Here I shoot once and pull the club.
  • It's small and light enough for a pants pocket. I walk, so a rangefinder that clips to a cart is useless to me — this one just rides in my pocket all round.
  • Battery life is real. Four months and roughly 30 rounds on the original battery, still going.
  • The display is readable in flat, dull light. That's the single thing my last one got wrong and it's the reason I stopped using it.

What I Didn't Like

  • The slope switch is too easy to bump. It's a small slider on the side, and it moved on its own in my bag more than once. Most junior tournaments require slope to be disabled — always check your event's rules, and check the switch on the first tee. I do it every round now out of paranoia.
  • No magnet. If you ride, it won't stick to the cart rail, and there's no strap included either.
  • $249 is real money for a device that doesn't hit a single shot for you. A junior playing a familiar home course does not need this.

Who It's For

Good Fit For

  • Juniors who play tournaments on courses they don't know
  • Walkers who want something pocket-sized
  • Players guessing yardages between sprinkler heads

Not a Great Fit For

  • Anyone who plays the same home course every week
  • Younger juniors who'd get more out of a simple GPS watch
  • Riders who want a cart-magnet rangefinder

Price & Value

$249. The two realistic alternatives: a free GPS app on your phone, and a basic $150 laser.

The phone app is fine for a casual round and useless in a tournament where you don't want to be pulling a phone out. The $150 lasers I've used are slower to lock, which sounds minor until you're the group holding everyone up. The R1 is worth the step up if you're playing competitively — otherwise the app is genuinely good enough, and I'd say that even though this one was free.

Final Verdict

4/5 — Very good. A very good tournament tool that earns its place in my pocket. The slope switch is a real flaw for junior competitors and the price is steep for casual players, so it's a 4 rather than a 5. If it were $199 with a locking slope switch, it'd be the easiest recommendation on this site.
