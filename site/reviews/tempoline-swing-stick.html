TempoLine Swing Stick Review: Is It Worth It for Junior Golfers?

Six weeks, three sessions a week, and I still can't point to one thing this changed. A rare low score on this site, and here's exactly why.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated April 2026 ·5 min read

Quick Take

I used this three times a week for six weeks and I can't point to a single thing it changed about my swing. The build quality is fine and it works as a warm-up club, but the feedback is so vague it stops telling you anything. $89 buys a lot of alignment sticks and range balls.

What It Is

The TempoLine Swing Stick is a weighted 44" training club with an internal slider that clicks at the top of your backswing and again at impact — the idea being that the gap between the two clicks trains a consistent tempo. There's a companion app that logs the swings.

How I Tested It

Six weeks, three sessions a week, mostly 15–20 swings before range practice and some indoor use at home. I also used it as a pre-round warm-up for four tournament rounds. I filmed my swing at the start and end of the six weeks with the same phone setup so I could compare.

What I Liked

  • It's well built. It's been thrown in a car trunk all spring and there's nothing loose or rattling.
  • It works as a warm-up club. Ten swings with something heavier and I feel loose before a round. That's a real thing — it's just not what this is sold as.
  • It breaks down to fit in a bag, so I did actually take it to tournaments instead of leaving it at home.

What I Didn't Like

  • The click fires on almost any swing. I deliberately made bad, rushed swings and it clicked happily. Feedback that never tells you "no" isn't feedback.
  • The app is a mess and there's a subscription. Pairing dropped constantly, and most of the drills sit behind $6/month that isn't mentioned anywhere on the box.
  • 44" is too long for shorter juniors indoors. My teammate couldn't swing it in her garage without hitting the ceiling.
  • A coach and a phone camera do this better, for free. Six weeks of video told me more than six weeks of clicking did.

To be clear about what I can and can't say: I'm one junior golfer with one swing, and six weeks isn't a scientific test. No training aid can promise you'll gain distance or improve your tempo, and I'm not claiming the opposite either — I'm telling you what happened when I used it.

Who It's For

Good Fit For

  • Someone who specifically wants a weighted warm-up club and finds this under about $40
  • Players without access to a coach who'll use the tempo idea as a starting point — with realistic expectations

Not a Great Fit For

  • Anyone hoping it fixes tempo on its own
  • Juniors under about 5'2", especially for indoor use
  • Anyone who already works with a coach — your phone camera is more useful
  • Anyone who'd rather put $89 into lessons or range time

Price & Value

$89, plus $6/month if you want the drills. Compare that to $15 for a pair of alignment sticks and a free swing-camera app — which is what I went back to — or to roughly one lesson with a coach, which for me was worth more than all six weeks of this.

There's a version of this product I'd like at $39 as a warm-up stick. At $89 with a subscription attached, the value isn't there.

Final Verdict

2/5 — Below average. Well made, and useless at the job it's sold for. It didn't change anything I could see on video, the feedback is too forgiving to teach you anything, and the subscription isn't disclosed where you'd see it before buying. Buy alignment sticks and put the rest toward a lesson.
