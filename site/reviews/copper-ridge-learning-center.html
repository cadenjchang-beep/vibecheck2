Copper Ridge Golf Learning Center Review: A Junior Golfer's Take

Grass tees every day of the week, a short game area with real bunkers, and a $60 junior membership that pays for itself in three weeks. The place my short game actually improved.

C By Caden, 15 — junior golfer ·Updated July 2026 ·6 min read

Quick Take

This is the best practice facility I've used, and the difference is that it's built for juniors instead of putting up with them. Grass tees every day, a short game area with real bunkers, and a junior membership that's cheap enough to actually use. Get there before 4pm on a weekday or you'll be waiting.

The Basics

  • Where: Brookvale, Washington. It's a practice facility, not a full course.
  • What's there: grass driving range, a 9-hole par 3 (1,150 yards, holes from 60 to 165), a three-green short game complex with two bunkers, and four covered heated bays.
  • Prices: $9 large bucket, $8 junior for the par 3 ($14 adult), $60/month junior unlimited range membership.
  • Walkability: everything is a two-minute walk from the parking lot.
  • Hours: open year round, lights until 9pm.

What Stood Out

  • Grass tees every day, not just weekends. This is the whole reason I drive 25 minutes to get here. Hitting off mats all winter taught me bad habits I spent a spring undoing.
  • The short game area is the real thing. Three greens with different slopes and two bunkers with consistent sand. I got noticeably better out of sand this spring and it happened here.
  • The par 3 is a proper wedge classroom. Holes from 60 to 165 yards means you hit every scoring club you own in 90 minutes, and $8 for juniors is nothing.
  • Covered heated bays mean January practice actually happens instead of being a thing I plan and then skip.
  • $60/month junior unlimited. If you go twice a week it pays for itself in three weeks. That number changed how much I practice.

Challenges

  • 4pm to 7pm on weekdays it's packed. Show up at 5 and you're waiting for a bay, sometimes 20 minutes.
  • Range balls get beaten up by Sunday. By the end of a busy weekend the distances stop being reliable, so I don't gap-test late in the week.
  • There's no full-length course, so you can't practice actual course management or hitting driver into a real fairway.
  • The parking lot is too small for how busy it gets.

Junior-Friendliness

Best I've seen anywhere, and it isn't close.

  • Junior rates: on everything — buckets, the par 3, the membership.
  • Saturday morning junior clinics run most of the year and they're not just for beginners.
  • Nobody hassles you for spending an hour chipping without buying a bucket, which at most places gets you a look.
  • Staff will actually help. I've had someone come out and fix my ball position without being asked, twice.
  • Safe to get dropped off at — it's small, it's all visible from the parking lot, and there's a covered area to wait in.

Best For

Show up for

  • Tournament prep, especially short game
  • Winter practice when everything else is mats or mud
  • Juniors without a home club
  • Parents who want a drop-off practice spot

Play somewhere else if

  • You need to practice full-course strategy — go play 18 somewhere
  • The only time you can go is 5pm on a weekday
  • You want a driving range with a bar and screens; this isn't that

Final Verdict

5/5 — Exceptional. Five stars, and I don't hand those out. The grass tees, the bunkers and the $60 junior membership together make this the single most useful place in my golf life right now. The crowding and the tired range balls are real annoyances — they're just not enough to change what this place is worth to a junior who wants to get better.
