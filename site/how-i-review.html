The policy

How I review

Short version: I have to actually use it, I have to say what I didn't like, and if someone gave it to me for free, you find out before you read a single opinion.

What counts as tested

A review doesn't go up until I've used the thing enough to have a real opinion. My own minimums:

  • Clubs: at least 5 rounds and 5 range sessions, in more than one kind of weather.
  • Balls: at least 4 rounds, including putting and short game, plus a few shots I intentionally hit badly.
  • Bags, shoes, gloves: at least a month of normal use, including a walking round in rain if I can get one.
  • Rangefinders and GPS: at least 5 rounds, with at least one tournament round.
  • Training aids: at least 6 weeks of regular use.
  • Courses: at least one full 18, walked if walking is allowed. If I've only played 9, the review says so.

Every review lists exactly how much testing it got. If the number looks small to you, weigh the review accordingly — that's why it's there.

The rating scale

Same five points on every review, gear or course. I try to keep the middle of the scale meaningful instead of making everything a 4 or 5.

5/5

Exceptional. I'd recommend it without hesitation. Still has to list downsides — nothing is perfect.

4/5

Very good, with minor drawbacks.

3/5

Solid. Does the job, but has real trade-offs you should know about before you buy.

2/5

Below average. Hard to recommend at the price.

1/5

Skip it.

A 5-star review with no listed downside doesn't exist here. If I can't find one, I haven't used it long enough.

Free gear, discounts and disclosure

Sometimes a brand sends a product, or a course comps a round. When that happens:

  • It's disclosed in a box at the top of the review, before any opinion.
  • Nobody gets to read the review before it goes up, and nobody gets to ask for changes.
  • The rating scale doesn't move. A free 2-star product still gets 2 stars.
  • I don't take payment for a rating, a placement, or a promise of coverage.

If a review has no disclosure box, my family bought it or I already owned it.

Things this site will never do

  • Fake testing. If I haven't hit it or played it, I don't write about it.
  • Health or safety claims. No product here "prevents injury" or "guarantees" you'll gain distance. I'm a golfer, not a doctor or a coach — for anything about your body or your swing mechanics, talk to a PGA professional or a medical professional.
  • Trash-talking other brands. I'll compare products directly, but only on things I actually measured or felt, and only against gear I've used.
  • Grown-up content. No gambling, no 19th-hole stuff. Most of the people reading this are under 18.
  • Inflated ratings for free product. See above.

Links and money

If a review ever contains an affiliate link, it will be labelled, and it will never change what the review says or what the rating is. The plan is to keep this site's income and its opinions completely separate.

For brands and courses

Happy to hear from you. What I can promise: an honest review, published on my schedule, with disclosure. What I can't promise: a rating, a publish date, or that you'll like it. If that works, get in touch.

Questions people ask

You're 15 — why should I trust your reviews?

Because I'm the person the gear is built for. I'm not going to tell you about launch angle databases or tour-level spin numbers. I'll tell you whether a driver still felt swingable on the 34th hole of a two-round tournament day, and whether the bag strap dug into my shoulder. For lab data, read the big equipment sites — I read them too.

Do you get paid for reviews?

No. Some products are sent free, which is always disclosed at the top of the review. No brand pays for coverage or for a rating.

Can you review the gear I'm thinking about buying?

Send me a note — suggestions genuinely shape what gets reviewed next. I can only review what I can get my hands on, so it might take a while.

Why is a 3-star review still worth reading?

Because 3 stars usually means "good product, wrong for some people." The trade-off section tells you which group you're in. A lot of the gear I actually recommend to teammates is 3-star gear at the right price.
